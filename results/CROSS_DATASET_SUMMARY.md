The two run-to-failure datasets (XJTU-SY, Paderborn RTF) mostly measure life stage (a life-stage-only detector scores 84.9 on XJTU-SY; the pre-declared label permutation control exceeded its 56 threshold on both); treat them as weak evidence. B1 recipes: PU, UORED and CWRU from Vieira et al.'s configs (probably tuned by them on those datasets), PU recipe for XJTU-SY and Paderborn RTF. Own B1 re-implementation (about +3 above the published value on PU, -2.8 below on CWRU).
What the data support: rowz_drop is at least as good as base in all five datasets (point estimates +2.0 to +10.6, significant on UORED and CWRU) and above B1 in four of five (significant on PU, CWRU and Paderborn RTF); B1 is ahead on UORED (not significant for rowz_drop, significant for base). The speed-variation explanation (our order-resampled input helps when speed varies) is a hypothesis, not a result: it fits PU and Paderborn RTF and UORED, but CWRU (constant speed) does not fit it. A weak outer-race bearing exists in every dataset with outer-race labels (PU KA15/KA22/KA30, UORED O8, CWRU DE_OR_14, Paderborn B01/B11/B16 with inner damage, XJTU 3_1/3_2); the cause was not investigated.

## Corrections and additions to the summary above
- Gate G2 (replication on at least two independent datasets, among other criteria) is NOT met. PU is the development dataset (rowz_drop was designed on its tuning splits), so it is not independent evidence; the two run-to-failure sets are confounded by life stage (below); the only independent dataset with a clean one-state-per-bearing label design is CWRU (artificial faults); UORED is independent but its three states per bearing are life stages of the same bearing (negatives for each label include faulty windows of other fault types, which limits but does not remove the stage cue). The earlier wording "H2 supported on at least two datasets" must not be read as meeting G2.
- Stage-only reference detectors (score = window comes from a faulty life stage; no fault-type information; `scripts/audit/stage_only_reference.py`, XJTU-SY from `results/xjtu/null_life_stage.json`): UORED 70.0 (models 80.9 / 91.6 / 94.2: all above), XJTU-SY 84.9 (models 74.1 / 76.0 / 69.8: all below), Paderborn run-to-failure 84.1 (models 76.4 / 78.9 / 72.5: all below). On the two run-to-failure sets no model reaches the stage-only reference, so their headline scores cannot support a claim of fault-type discrimination; only the pre-declared stage-matched control (negatives restricted to late-life windows of bearings without the defect: XJTU-SY rowz_drop 81.3 vs B1 67.5, Paderborn run-to-failure rowz_drop 72.96 vs B1 66.10 vs base 68.52) addresses fault type, and it was a control analysis, not a decision rule.
- The label-permutation controls on those two sets measure stage learning, not leakage (no bearing is on both sides of any split); the whole-bearing permutation on Paderborn run-to-failure (about 49) was added after the first control behaved unexpectedly and is a post hoc analysis.
- B1 recipes for PU, CWRU and UORED are the benchmark authors' own configurations (probably tuned by them), for XJTU-SY and Paderborn run-to-failure the untuned PU recipe; B1 is an own re-implementation; no stronger or different baseline (for example random forests on handcrafted features) was run on the four external datasets.
- Status of the stage-matched analysis: it was declared as a descriptive analysis (no decision rule) in PREDECLARED_XJTU.txt (16:43) and PREDECLARED_RTF.txt (16:06), before any model was trained on those sets. The stage-only reference numbers and the whole-bearing permutation control are post hoc.
- Robustness of the stage-matched analysis to a mislabelled negative bearing (`scripts/audit/stage_matched_robustness.py`, point estimates, leave-one-negative-bearing-out): XJTU-SY rowz_drop minus B1 between +8.5 and +17.2 and rowz_drop minus base between +1.3 and +8.7 over all 13 dropped bearings; Paderborn run-to-failure rowz_drop minus B1 between +5.6 and +9.7 and rowz_drop minus base between +2.3 and +6.1 over 14 dropped bearings. This does not address systematic mislabelling of all negatives.
- Who defined the life-stage labelling: for UORED the dataset authors (healthy = first 5 files, developing = files 25-30, faulty = files 45-50 of one run); for XJTU-SY and Paderborn run-to-failure the labelling (first and last 10%) is this project's own rule, so the stage-shortcut observation is a limit of this protocol, not a finding about other work.
- Wording to use: "no evidence of deterioration (point estimates positive in all five datasets, significant in two; the lower confidence bounds are above -4.0 points)", not "equal"; no equivalence margin was defined.

## Second baseline: random forest on 20 handcrafted features (see `rf/SUMMARY.md`)
Macro-AUROC, random forest (RF) versus base / rowz_drop / B1: CWRU 80.35 vs 88.40 / 95.29 / 71.75; UORED 94.43 vs 80.92 / 91.55 / 94.21; XJTU-SY 79.77 vs 74.07 / 76.04 / 69.84; Paderborn run-to-failure 85.70 vs 76.42 / 78.85 / 72.51. rowz_drop minus RF: +14.94 [+7.52, +23.32] (CWRU), -2.88 [-6.74, +0.55] (UORED), -3.73 [-6.84, -0.28] (XJTU-SY), -6.85 [-12.72, -1.69] (Paderborn). On PU the shallow baselines are far below (B2 69.72, B3 66.76 versus 85.63 and 88.85). With life stage matched, rowz_drop and RF are tied on both run-to-failure sets (XJTU-SY 81.34 vs 80.11; Paderborn 72.96 vs 72.76). On the run-to-failure sets RF is at the level of the stage-only reference and its time statistics carry that signal (post hoc diagnostic); on UORED five time statistics alone reach 94.2. Consequence: the neural order-spectrum model is clearly better than a shallow baseline only on PU and CWRU; on UORED it is behind or tied, on the run-to-failure sets tied after stage matching.
