EXTERNAL VALIDATION ON XJTU-SY (accelerated-life run-to-failure bearings, Xi'an Jiaotong University / Changxing Sumyoung; Wang, Lei, Li, Li, IEEE Trans. Reliability 69(1):401-412, 2020).
Written BEFORE any window was extracted and before any model was trained on this data. Not to be changed afterwards. Any later pipeline error: fix, rerun everything, report both versions.

Purpose: frozen test of LF-PNCN (`base`) and of the phase 2 change `rowz_drop` against B1 (WDCNN on the fixed-Hz envelope spectrum) on REAL, naturally developed damage at CONSTANT speed within each bearing (three nearby conditions: 2100, 2250, 2400 rpm). Nothing on this dataset is used to choose or change any method.

PREDICTION (stated before the result, not a result): on UORED (constant speed, natural damage) the fixed-Hz B1 beat base and tied rowz_drop, while on PU and CWRU the LF-PNCN family beat B1; the working explanation is speed variation. XJTU-SY has constant speed inside every bearing and only three close speeds across bearings (35, 37.5, 40 Hz), so B1 is expected to be COMPETITIVE here (H2 not supported or inconclusive for rowz_drop; no clear LF-PNCN-family advantage over B1). If rowz_drop clearly beats B1 here, the speed-variation explanation is not supported by this dataset. No prediction is made for U2.

SOURCE FACTS (all from the dataset authors' documents in work/data/xjtu/docs, read in full; see results/datasets/XJTU_SY_PROVENANCE.md): 15 bearings LDK UER204, three conditions with five bearings each (35 Hz / 12 kN, 37.5 Hz / 11 kN, 40 Hz / 10 kN = 2100, 2250, 2400 rpm); two PCB 352C33 accelerometers (horizontal and vertical), 25.6 kHz, 1.28 s (32768 samples) every minute, one csv per sample, column 1 horizontal, column 2 vertical; failure when the amplitude exceeds 20 g. Failure location per bearing (authors' Table 3): 1_1 outer, 1_2 outer, 1_3 outer, 1_4 cage, 1_5 inner+outer, 2_1 inner, 2_2 outer, 2_3 cage, 2_4 outer, 2_5 outer, 3_1 outer, 3_2 inner+rolling element+cage+outer, 3_3 inner, 3_4 inner, 3_5 outer. Geometry (authors' Table 1): ball diameter 7.92 mm, pitch diameter 34.55 mm, 8 balls, contact angle 0 deg (inner raceway 29.30 mm, outer raceway 39.80 mm); BPFO/f_r = 3.083, BPFI/f_r = 4.917 (computed by the code). The authors' document reports, for bearing 1_1, a measured shaft frequency of 34.38 Hz (nominal 35 Hz) and an outer-race fault frequency near 108.6 Hz (theory 107.91 Hz), which supports the geometry. The authors state that the horizontal signal contains more degradation information because the load acts horizontally. Local data: only the first and last 10% of every bearing's files (at least 5 files each; work/data/xjtu/selection_first_last_10pct.json; 1862 files; all checked finite and of shape 32768 x 2); nothing else exists locally and nothing is downloaded.

DATA HANDLING CHOSEN A PRIORI (same for all three models):
 1. Channel: horizontal (column 1), chosen a priori on the authors' statement above.
 2. Snapshot selection (label-free): healthy-stage snapshots = the first m files of a bearing, faulty-stage snapshots = the last m files, m = max(5, ceil(10% of the number of files N)) as extracted (the 80% in between is a guard band and is not available locally). At most 100 snapshots per end per bearing, evenly spaced by file index (np.linspace rounded), so that bearings with thousands of snapshots do not dominate (3_1, 3_2, 3_4 are capped at 100 per end; 3_1 and 3_2 have 254 and 250 files per end).
 3. Labels: healthy-stage windows are 0 for IR and OR. Faulty-stage windows carry the Table 3 label of their bearing: IR = 1 for 2_1, 3_3, 3_4, 1_5, 3_2 (5 bearings; 2_1, 3_3, 3_4 are inner-only); OR = 1 for 1_1, 1_2, 1_3, 2_2, 2_4, 2_5, 3_1, 3_5, 1_5, 3_2 (10 bearings; 8 are outer-only). The cage-only bearings 1_4 and 2_3 are negative for IR and OR at both ends (ball and cage components of 3_2 are not targets; they are handled like ball faults on PU and UORED). The defect onset of every bearing is unknown, so some faulty-end windows may precede the documented defect (label noise, a limitation); for bearings with a very short life (1_5: 52 files, 2_4: 42 files) the end windows are 5 or 6 files and may be close to each other in time.
 4. Window: the FIRST 25,600 samples (1 s) of the 1.28-s snapshot, resampled to 42 kHz with scipy.signal.resample_poly(105, 64) = 42000 samples, so that the frozen LF-PNCN front end (envelope bands 500-2500, 2500-5000, 5000-10000 Hz, 5 harmonics, 64 xi points, Delta rule) runs unchanged. The resampling adds no information (the sensor band is limited by the 12.8 kHz Nyquist of the original data; the 10 kHz band edge is below it).
 5. Shaft frequency: the nominal frequency of the condition folder (35, 37.5, 40 Hz). A label-free check is run on all selected files before any training (results/xjtu/speed_check.txt: peak of the Welch spectrum of the horizontal channel within +-5% of the nominal frequency, resolution 0.78 Hz). Rule fixed now: nominal frequency is used for a folder unless the median measured shaft-line frequency of that folder differs from nominal by more than 2.5%; in that case the folder median replaces the nominal frequency for the whole folder (constant per folder). The outcome is recorded in speed_check.txt; the rule is not otherwise changed.
 6. Geometry: authors' Table 1 as above (z=8, d=7.92 mm, D=34.55 mm, contact angle 0).
 7. LF-PNCN variants: `base` and `rowz_drop` exactly as in scripts/phase2/variants.py, PU Tier 1 configuration (lr 1e-3, batch 64, weight decay 1e-4, 40 epochs), seed = split number, standardiser from the training windows only, input = the frozen t_mad tensor; Ball output excluded from the loss; two labels [IR, OR]. Trained on the CPU (2 threads per process).
 8. B1: own WDCNN implementation (work/scripts/b1: envelope spectrum band-pass 500-10000 Hz order 12 on the 42 kHz window, 21000 bins, WDCNN with 2 outputs [IR, OR], BCE-with-logits), PU recipe: Adam lr 1e-3, batch 32, 30 epochs, per-window gain |N(1, 0.3)|, global normalisation = division by the std of the raw (resampled) samples of the offset-0 training windows, seed = split number, last epoch scored on the offset-0 windows (the same windows as LF-PNCN). DEVIATIONS (declared in advance): (a) true random crops are replaced by three fixed crop starts per snapshot (0, 0.14 s and 0.28 s = samples 0, 3584 and 7168 of the 32768-sample snapshot; each crop is 25600 samples, then resampled to 42 kHz); each epoch draws 864 (window, offset) pairs without replacement from the training pool (27 batches of 32 = 810 optimisation steps per split, comparable to the PU recipe of about 27 batches x 30 epochs); in the sensitivity analysis min(864, pool) rounded down to a multiple of 32; (b) the std comes from the selected training windows; (c) 2 outputs; (d) B1 is trained on the GPU (memory kept below about 500 MB). Caveat: this is the PU recipe, not tuned for this dataset (the LF-PNCN family is not tuned either).
 9. Splits: 100 bearing-wise splits, 10 training and 5 test bearings, drawn uniformly without replacement from the set of all C(15,5) = 3003 test sets that satisfy: the test set contains at least 1 OR-positive and at least 1 IR-positive bearing and the training set contains at least 2 OR-positive and at least 2 IR-positive bearings (2699 of 3003 satisfy it). Seed numpy default_rng(20261029); split numbers 5..104 (results/xjtu/splits.csv); the same splits for all models. All windows of a bearing (healthy and faulty end) are on the same side.
 10. Metric: per split, the pooled-window AUROC of the IR score and of the OR score over all windows of the five test bearings (positives = faulty-end windows with that defect label; negatives = every other window of the test bearings, including their healthy-end windows and the windows of bearings that lack that defect); Macro = mean of the two; mean over the 100 splits.
 11. Statistics: paired bearing-level cluster bootstrap, B = 2000, same draws for all models (seed 0); the cluster is the physical bearing (each bearing contributes its healthy and faulty windows); resampling with replacement inside three strata (the 5 IR-positive bearings, the 8 OR-only bearings, the 2 cage-only bearings); weighted-AUROC fast path as in scripts/uored/analyze_uored.py and scripts/rtf/analyze_rtf.py, verified against sklearn; 95% percentile intervals; splits in which a label loses all its positives or negatives are skipped for that replicate.
 12. Sensitivity (reported for all three models): the complete pipeline (training and test windows, same splits, same seeds, same model settings) restricted to a 5% rule: only snapshots in the first and last ceil(5% of N) files of each bearing (at least 3 files) among the primary windows. It is not a decision criterion.
 13. Descriptive analyses (no decision rule): per-bearing AUROC for every IR-positive and OR-positive bearing (faulty windows of that bearing against all negatives, and against late-life negatives, of the splits in which it is a test bearing); and a LIFE-STAGE CONTROL: the same Macro-style AUROC with negatives restricted to the faulty-end windows of bearings that lack the defect (for OR: the late windows of the IR-only bearings 2_1, 3_3, 3_4 and the cage-only bearings 1_4, 2_3; for IR: the late windows of the 8 OR-only bearings and the 2 cage-only bearings), which tests fault-type discrimination independently of "early versus late life".
 14. Negative controls (all three models): (i) bearing-label permutation on the first 20 splits (5..24), 2 permutations each: the (IR, OR) faulty-end label pair of each of the 15 bearings is reassigned to a different bearing at random (healthy windows stay 0; redrawn at most 20 times until every training and test set still has both classes for both labels); expected held-out Macro about 50; a mean above 56 for any model would indicate leakage or identity memorisation; (ii) train-versus-held-out gap: the window-level Macro-AUROC on the training windows is recorded in every split and reported next to the held-out value.

PRE-STATED DECISION RULES (reported whatever the outcome):
 U1: each variant's Macro bootstrap 95% lower bound > 50.
 U2: rowz_drop minus base: confirmed if > 0 with the CI lower bound > 0; inconclusive if the point estimate >= 0 and the CI includes 0; not reproduced if < 0.
 H2: for each of rowz_drop and base versus B1: supported if the difference > 0 with the CI lower bound > 0; inconclusive if the point estimate >= 0 and the CI includes 0; not supported if the point estimate < 0; the literal +6 point threshold is reported as met or not met on the point estimate.
 No hyperparameter or variant is changed, added or removed after seeing results.

KNOWN LIMITS AND CAVEATS (stated in advance): labels of run-to-failure data are defined by early and late life, so a model may partly learn life stage instead of fault type (controlled only by the guard band and by the late-life-negatives analysis in item 13; NOT controlled: drifts of vibration level, load, temperature and mounting with age, differences between bearings); the three condition groups (speed and load) are confounded with bearing identity (condition 3 contains the long-lived bearings and three of the five IR-positive bearings); only 5 IR-positive bearings (3 inner-only) and 10 OR-positive bearings (8 outer-only); cage-only bearing 1_4 changes little in vibration RMS between early and late life (0.42 to 0.60 in the first and last 10%), so its faulty windows are close to healthy ones; the defect onset is unknown; speeds are nominal (no tachometer); the load is very high (10-12 kN); the data are the two ends of each life only; the geometry is from the authors' document but the contact angle 0 is their stated value; results are not comparable with other papers (different labels, windows and splits).
